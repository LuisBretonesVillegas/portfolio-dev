Four months ago I decided I was going to finish my degree with the CCNA. At that point my university finals were only a few weeks away, so I put off planning it until they were over.

Then over the summer I put together a roadmap, a kind of outline of what I needed to finish my degree with a portfolio that gets recruiters interested in my profile. I concluded that the order was CCNA -> RHCSA as the essentials, with the rest depending on the path I choose later on. I also set myself the goal of finishing my degree with a B1 in German, which together with my C1 in English makes it viable for me to aim at the international market.

In the first version of this roadmap I planned one certification per year, studying for the CCNA and RHCSA alongside my degree and arriving at my company internship with both. What I didn't take into account was how much time I'd have once classes started.

Every class day adds up to 4 hours of travel between my home and the university, so I had much less time than I'd expected.

That's when I realized I wasn't going to make it to the CCNA before February. It was impossible, so I replanned the roadmap. Instead of taking the exam before it changes to version v2.0, I went for a much more relaxed approach, where I finish preparing in June and take the exam between August and October, depending on how well my theory review goes.

Then I realized that if I take it in February 2028, I can do it with a 70% discount from my university. That obviously changed my decision, and I moved the exam to the end of February.

Finishing preparing for an exam 8 months before taking it? It sounds crazy, but it makes sense. UAL offers a 70% discount to anyone who passes the Netacad exam under a professor's supervision. Three courses in my degree cover the three parts of the CCNA, and the last one, Network Architecture and Operating Systems, will serve as active review. Add the Anki flashcards from Jeremy's IT Lab, and in those 8 months I won't forget what I've learned, I'll reinforce it.

My mistake wasn't wanting to get the CCNA, it was planning for it without considering that the day only has 24 hours. Now that I'm taking into account my university schedule and the study time it requires, I'll be able to secure this certification in February 2028.
